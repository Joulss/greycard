# §501. The engine, part two: what a kept point carries

*2026-11-01*

Before the window's develop and the open file's export move onto one
Kept (§478's last landing, split in three), the walk had to be right
about what a kept point knows. Four things it did not.

**The stand-in.** Why the learned denoiser stood aside lived on the
walk's result, so a walk that resumed past the stand-in's points
reported nothing. It lives in the point's Meta now, written on every
walk, since the stand-in's list is the same one an edit without the
learned denoiser makes and their points serve each other: a plain
edit resumed from a stand-in's points says none. A denoiser not to
be had is asked for before the walk starts, so the list is the
engine's own from its first step, the mosaic is prepared once, and
the next walk resumes from the stand-in's points.

**The unmade fills.** Which Fill patches were left as they were
travels with the kept Patched point, refusals included, so a walk
that starts past the retouch still makes its masks on the right
picture and the export's report is right. The export no longer keeps
a list of its own. The fills are said under the job's generation.

**A demosaic that gave way.** The learned stage clears it itself,
since the network is the demosaic; the export's filter on Ran or
Cached is gone.

**The resume key.** Kept points are matched by the picture their
steps make: keeps are skipped on both sides, and what each purpose
makes of the learned masks (Make, or OnAsk) reads alike. So an
export's list resumes from the viewport's points where those are on
the CPU, with the planes, the upload, and the haze compared the same
way. A resumed point starts at the walk's own Keep and is re-put
under the same Arc, no pixels copied.

**Held for 3b and 3c.** Any Meta field the stage list does not
determine is a staleness risk on resume. A turned Base clears the
masks tap, so a walk resumed from it makes no masks. An export
resuming from the window's Kept must not retain or clear the window's
points: a read-only resume, or a borrowed copy. The bar was 40 of 40
byte-equal on a clean run; one earlier run differed on one learned
mask frame while two runs shared a cache folder, and did not
reproduce over eight reruns.
